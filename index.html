The Theological Foundation

Why this organization exists.

"For even when we were with you, we gave you this rule: The one who is unwilling to work shall not eat."

2 Thessalonians 3:10 (NIV)

This verse is not a suggestion. The Greek word Paul uses — παραγγέλλω — is a military term for a binding command. Those who are able and willing to work are obligated to work. This has been the position of the Christian tradition from Paul through Luther through Calvin to the present day.

But the mandate has a second edge that the tradition has sometimes overlooked. If those who are willing to work must work, then the community of faith has a corresponding obligation: to ensure that those who are willing have the capacity to do so. When technology displaces a worker from their vocation, the Church does not get to offer thoughts and prayers. The Church is obligated to equip.

"Do you see someone skilled in their work? They will serve before kings; they will not serve before officials of low rank."

Proverbs 22:29 (NIV)

This is the standard we hold ourselves to. We do not train people to barely qualify for entry-level positions. We train people to stand before kings — to carry credentials that employers actively seek, skills that genuinely transfer, and confidence that comes from genuine mastery.

The Thessalonian Mandate Institute is not a charitable service with religious language applied to it. It is a ministry — an organization that understands workforce retraining as an act of obedience to divine command — and it operates with the theological rigor that obligation demands.

Our Model

Lean by design. Powerful by partnership.

The Institute does not build curriculum. It builds pipelines. By partnering with IBM, Google, Microsoft, and Khan Academy — platforms that have already invested billions in free workforce training infrastructure — we deliver world-class instruction at zero cost to participants and minimal cost to the organization. Our role is to recruit, assess, support, and place. That is where human ministry happens. That is where your investment goes.

01
Recruit and Assess

We find displaced workers through community partnerships, workforce boards, and faith networks. We assess their baseline and match them to the pathway with the highest probability of successful employment.

02
Train and Support

Participants train through free platforms — Google, IBM, Microsoft, Khan Academy — in cohorts of 8–15. Weekly check-ins, peer accountability, and navigation support ensure completion rates that free platforms alone cannot achieve.

03
Credential and Place

Graduates earn industry-recognized credentials from IBM, Google, and Microsoft. We connect them directly to employer networks, workforce boards, and job fairs — and we advocate for them until they are employed.

Our Founder

Built by someone who lived the problem.

The Thessalonian Mandate Institute was not founded in a boardroom. It was founded by someone who spent twenty-three years navigating homelessness, instability, and a labor market that stripped away every foothold as fast as it could be found. Eleven of those years were spent as an itinerant street preacher — proclaiming the dignity of labor across the United States from the exact position of someone the economy had left behind.

The Institute exists because its founder eventually understood that the work mandate of scripture is not a comfort. It is a command — and commands create obligations. The obligation to equip the willing was not being fulfilled. She built the organization that should have existed when she needed it.

This is a ministry grounded in scripture, tested by experience, and built with the rigor that both demand. The founding documents — Theological Framework, Statement of Faith, and Bylaws — are available to any grantor upon request and reflect the full depth of the doctrinal convictions that drive this work.

Rickey Jay Bennett II
Founder · Minister · Chief Executive
